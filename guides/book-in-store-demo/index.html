How to book an in-store demo at an independent grocery store

What to have ready, how far ahead to book, and how to make the day worth it for you and the store.

An in-store demo is one of the best ways for a food or beverage brand to win a new shopper. Independent grocers are often glad to host one, and they have less staff and less time than a chain to manage it. The brands that get booked again are the ones that make it easy for the store. This guide covers what to have ready and what to expect.

What to have ready before you ask

  • A current certificate of insurance. Nearly every store asks for one showing general liability coverage. Your broker can send it the same day. See our guide to demo insurance for the details.
  • The exact items you will sample, with the item name, UPC and the distributor the store orders from. The store uses this to make sure your product is on the shelf.
  • Who will staff the table. Your own team or a demo agency, with a name and a phone number for the day.
  • Anything special you need, such as electricity for a warmer or a blender.

Book ahead

Most stores ask for a lead time, and two weeks is common. It is not red tape. The store needs that time to check stock and order more if needed. A demo for a product that sold out on Thursday does nobody any good on Saturday.

Pick the right slot

Weekend midday and late afternoon slots have the most shoppers. Ask the store which location and which slot suit your product. A store with several locations may have very different shoppers at each one.

Read the store's demo guidelines

Stores set their own rules on arrival time, setup, dress, food handling and cleanup. Read them before you book, and make sure whoever staffs the table has read them too. If your demo involves alcohol, cooking or open flame, raw or unpasteurized product, or CBD, ask first. Most stores allow those only with specific approval.

Know the fees and the cancellation rule

Some stores charge a demo fee and many do not. Ask what the fee is, when it is collected, and what happens if you have to cancel. A typical rule gives a full refund up to a few days before the demo.

On the day

  • Arrive early and check in with the manager on duty.
  • Confirm your product is on the shelf and note where it is, so you can point shoppers to it.
  • Keep the table clean and the samples small.
  • Leave the space the way you found it.

If you are a demo agency booking for several brands

Book each demo under the brand whose product is on the table. The store's calendar, its staff emails and its records all show the name on the booking. Mixing two brands under one name means the store orders the wrong product and the second brand has no record of its demo.

The short version: have your certificate and your item list ready, book two weeks out, read the store's guidelines, and make sure the product is on the shelf before you set up.
