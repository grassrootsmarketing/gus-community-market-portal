How to get your product into independent grocery stores

How independent grocers decide what to carry, what to bring to a buyer, and how to stay on the shelf.

Independent grocery stores are where many food and beverage brands get their start. The buyer is often a short walk from the shelf, decisions are made faster than at a chain, and a good local story counts for something. This guide covers how to approach them and how to keep your place once you are in.

Find the right person

At an independent store the decision usually sits with a category buyer or, at a small store, the owner. Ask at the store who buys for your category and how they like to be approached. Some want an email with a sell sheet. Some set aside a morning each week to meet new vendors.

Bring what a buyer needs to say yes

  • Samples. The product has to be tasted.
  • A one page sell sheet with your items, sizes, UPCs, case packs, wholesale price and suggested retail price.
  • How the store will get it. Either the distributor that carries you or your own delivery terms.
  • Your story in two sentences. Who makes it, where, and why a shopper at this store would pick it up.

Distributor or direct

Many independent grocers order most of their products through a natural foods distributor, and being in that distributor's catalog makes you easy to order. Getting into a distributor takes time, so plenty of new brands start by delivering direct to a handful of local stores. Ask each store which it prefers. Some are happy to take direct delivery from a local brand and some are not set up for it.

Know your numbers

Buyers think in terms of margin and how fast a product sells. Know your wholesale price, your suggested retail price and the margin that leaves the store. Be ready to say what you will do to help the product move in its first weeks.

Start small and local

Five stores where your product sells well are worth more than fifty where it sits. Strong sales at a few respected independents are also the best thing you can show the next buyer, or a distributor.

Use demos to earn your place on the shelf

Getting on the shelf is the start. Staying there depends on sales, and a new product nobody has tried sells slowly. An in-store demo puts it in a shopper's hand. Plan demos for the first weeks after your product arrives, and make sure it is in stock on the day. Our guide to booking an in-store demo covers what stores expect.

Follow up without being a pest

Check in after the first order. Ask what is selling and what is not, and fix what you can. Buyers remember the brands that make their job easier.

The short version: find the buyer, bring samples and a clear sell sheet, make ordering easy, start with a few stores, and use demos to get the product moving once it is on the shelf.
